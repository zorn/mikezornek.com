ElixirConf US 2026 happened last week in Chicago and while I was unable to attend in person, I did check out the festivities via a virtual ticket.

A virtual ticket pretty much means stream access to the talks live. There was no significant virtual attendee mingling, which feels like a missed opportunity.

Here are some talks that stood out for me. There is [a playlist on YouTube for the conference](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLVqsKC3zWaDc). Talks historically have been posted 6-9 months delayed for public consumption, with the exception of the keynotes however some non-keynote talks already seem to be up so IDK. If you have a ticket you can watch them all archived on the [Swapcard site](https://app.swapcard.com/event/elixirconf-us-2026).

## Recommended Talks  

**The Graveyard of Good Ideas by Quinn Wilton**

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ovlQ81rBc-4>

Wonderful talk with a mix of rich storytelling and deep technical ideas related to protocols, contracts, and developer tooling.

**Exoskeletons, not Autopilots by Zack Daniel**

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7smgEJkgqO8>

Zack recently took a new job working for a healthcare platform and shares a wide mix of how he has been using AI to help with coding and overall product heartbeats. I feel like I want to watch this again to further extract ideas for my own side project.

**It's Alive! Agent Self-Determination With Elixir (and NixOS) by Chris Ertel**

Chris explored some curious and somewhat scary ideas about how agents can self-propagate and customize live systems.

**A Dev Environment for Every Branch by Jason Axelson**

Jason shared his [Fast Worktree](https://github.com/axelson/fast-worktree) tooling, which he uses to solve common problems when trying to parallelize agent work. I've observed similar problems and will look to his code for inspiration. 

**Hologram: Building Local-First Apps in Pure Elixir by Bart Blast**

I enjoyed the chance to catch up and learn more about this library, which I've seen posting updates regularly. I'm not sure this aligns with my current local-first project goals, but it's good to know what else is out there.

**Lightning Talk: Brian Meeker shared his concept of Verified PubSub (similar to Verified Routes)** 

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vb1Rr9d6h9E&list=PLVqsKC3zWaDc>

I've always found PubSub to be one of the softer corners of Phoenix development, so the idea of having more compiler help to verify behavior sounds wonderful.

**Paying Down Tech Debt with AI by De Wet Blomerus**

Helpful review of how De Wet things about tech debt and some successes he has had utilizing AI to help address it. Aligns well with a lot of my recent blog writings. 

**Stop Routing; Start Flowing: Guided Workflows in LiveView by Matt Milton**

Matt shares his approach to building out complex workflows and wizards, explaining how complexity can bubble up from some nieave routing approaches. I agree heavily with his demo and how adding real state machines can drastically improve confidence and readability.

**What Expertise Looks Like by James Gray**

James (Elixir Book Club friend) talked through how he and his team solved some nasty large query issues. It serves as an example for how to think and solve large complex problems.
