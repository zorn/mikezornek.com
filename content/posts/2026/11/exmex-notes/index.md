This week I was a first-time attendee at [ExMex](https://exmexconf.com/), a single-track Elixir conference in Austin, Texas.

This is a smaller, more intimate event (~40-50 people), and small events are just as enjoyable to me as the larger ones. The smaller venue creates a more unique social experience, and this one had a great mix of introductions and connections with existing online friends for me.

I did not do a full talk, but I did present a short lightning talk on my journaling habits. Those lightning talks are not officially recorded, so I recorded a recreation of mine afterward. Here it is:

The 2026 ExMex videos should be posted in time. The [2025 videos are on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL3iGXu2WbjmuE2D5B2SA42-oz3NQi0f8E), so I would subscribe to that channel if you want to check them out when posted.

The talks were great, and here are a few personal notes:

- [James Edward Gray II](https://bsky.app/profile/jeg2.me), whom I know via the [Elixir Book Club](https://elixirbookclub.github.io/website/), did the opening keynote with a wonderful collection of stories, including his fascination with NASA. He gleefully and with admiration suggested that there were many weirdos in the audience. He compared the challenges of NASA to the challenges we face today as skilled programmers in the young world of AI: "we need the weirdos to figure this stuff out."
- [Chris Bell](https://x.com/cjbell_) of [Knock](https://knock.app/) shared some fascinating notes and observations from his work optimizing agents through a virtual filesystem. In the hallway track he also sourced a bunch of other agent things I need to look into.
- Chris Ertel followed up his S-tier ElixirConf 2026 talk ([It's Alive! Agent Self-Determination With Elixir (and NixOS)](https://elixirconf.com/participants/chris-ertel/)) with a history tour of the pivotal moments that shaped the tech industry, with the goal of pontificating about where we might be over the next 1, 5, and 10 years. I spent most of the talk nodding my head in agreement with the majority of the past influences and felt very aligned with his crystal ball. In the hallway track we talked about the unknown unknowns that are sure to come.
- [Amos King](https://github.com/adkron) walked us through some advanced property-based testing concepts, using fairly digestible scenario visuals to explain the differences between sequential consistency and linearizability. I have not done much property-based testing, though I did read some of the [Property-Based Testing book](https://pragprog.com/titles/fhproper/property-based-testing-with-proper-erlang-and-elixir/) a while back. With AI help it is probably easier to approach now, and it is something I'd love to check out again.
- [Brian Meeker](https://brianmeeker.me/) walked us through his new [VerifiedPubSub](https://hex.pm/packages/verified_pubsub) tool. I love the idea of getting more help from the compiler here, and it is something I want to check out.
- [Thomas Millar](https://thmsmlr.com/) of Anthropic built out a demo where people visit a website on their phones, and the demo then uses a browser-sourced BEAM to distribute processing power. Overall he wants to inspire people to be ambitious when thinking about how to use these AI tools.
- [Isaac Yonemoto](https://github.com/ityonemo) walked us through his recent past and his [new startup](https://vidalalabs.com/), which hopes to bring more sustainable manufacturing to pharmaceuticals. He demoed his custom hardware for sample analysis, and it was very impressive. I took special note of his use of [Scenic](https://github.com/ScenicFramework/scenic) for the touch screen interface.
- Michael Lubas of [Paraxial.io](https://paraxial.io/) (also a sponsor of ExMex, thank you!) talked about his security work with Hex ([ElixirConf video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_fER9bzlSOE) for a deep dive) and also shared some [skills he has been working on for Elixir security](https://github.com/paraxialio/skills).
- [Owen Bickford](https://owencode.com/) shared recent updates to his passkey work in [WebauthnComponents](https://github.com/liveshowy/webauthn_components).

Those are some personal picks, but I appreciated all the speakers and the diverse collection of topics covered. My notebook is overflowing with scribbles of things to check out and ideas to explore.

Thanks again to the ExMex organizers at [ConvivialTech](https://convivialtech.com/) for all their hard work, and to everyone else who came by to participate. I had a blast.
